## 1. How the user works

The user's three rules:

1. **No confident claims without evidence.** Don't report that something
   works, or that a problem is fixed, without having checked it. "We don't
   do sales here, we do engineering." If you make confident claims without
   evidence, those who listen will have no confidence in anything you say.
2. **Don't agree on a path and then do something else without the user
   knowing.** Ideas, suggestions, deviations and changes of plan are
   welcome, to be **shared** first. This doesn't matter for small things;
   it matters greatly for higher-level plans.
3. **Don't get lost in incremental fixing.** A problem, a "fix", a variant
   of it, another fix: passing tests can feel like progress while being an
   incremental loop that goes nowhere, because the big picture was lost.
   At each new variant of a problem, step back and reassess from first
   principles. This is worst when combined with rule 2.

The user's standing preferences:

- **Contribute ideas about everything**, the whole game, not only the task
  at hand, wacko ideas included, each assessed honestly.
- **Performance is on a par with the look; the look slightly ahead.** The
  combat and gameplay wanted need high frame rates (at least 100 fps on
  the user's machine: RTX 5060, 1080p) and low input lag. A striking,
  atmospheric look is the game's identity.
- **The project is experimental, designed as it goes.** A static world and
  the suns' orbits are not commitments, and good elements are sometimes
  found by accident.
- **Check for another `game.exe` before any run.** Run only one at a time
  (two at once have crashed the driver), and don't build or run heavy
  checks while the user is testing.
- **`--set mute on`** in test and capture runs.
- **Use `target/fast/game.exe`** (`cargo build -p game --profile fast`).

## 2. The project

null is an experimental FPS in Rust and Bevy 0.19. The world is a
procedurally generated, airless, greyscale world of flat polygonal plates
and megastructures, lit by two distant suns. The art direction is De
Chirico and Piranesi set against BLAME!. `README.md` covers the keys,
switches and places.

- **`crates/worldgen`**: engine-agnostic generation.
  - `plates.rs` (~3,000 lines): the ground. Three nested levels of
    Voronoi plates (`GRID` = 128, 32 and 8 m). Each piece is a convex
    prism with a flat top and vertical walls. A plate's height is built
    up down its chain of sites by its district's rules (`plate_raw`):
    landform, quantising, mesas and pits, tilts, pillars, wells and sites.
  - `district.rs`: the districts' rules.
  - `canal.rs`, `sites.rs`, `forms.rs`, `structure.rs`, `lattice.rs`: the
    canals, sites and structures.
  - `mesh.rs`: `ColumnMesh` and the column sizes.
- **`crates/game`**: the Bevy app.
  - `terrain.rs`: column streaming with levels of detail.
  - `terrain_vertex.wgsl`, `terrain_prepass.wgsl`: the planet bend.
  - `terrain.wgsl`: the ground's shading.
  - `look.rs`: suns, fill, bounce, cascades.
  - `sun_shadows.wgsl`: the game's copy of Bevy's shadows shader.
  - `far_shadow.rs`: the far maps.
  - `shell.rs`: the shell mask.
  - `rt_shadow.rs`: a ray-traced shadow spike, `--opt rt`.
  - `structures.rs`, plus `chasm/`, `combat/`, `player/`, `rig.rs`,
    `navmesh/` and more.
- **`docs/`**: `performance.md`, `world.md`, `engine-decision.md`,
  `removed.md` and others.
- **`TODO.md`**: the user's single review list of pending work.
- **`places.txt`**: named spots for `--tp NAME`.

**The current focus is sun shadows and the terrain's levels of detail.**
Combat, creatures and navigation wait on the world, and the world waits on
this.

## 4. What is being rendered

- **Per pixel** **[code]** (`terrain.wgsl`, `look.rs`): albedo × (Σ over
  suns of V(p) · max(N·L, 0) · E, plus a shadowless fill, bounce and
  lamps), plus glow, then haze. The ambient is near black, so an error in
  V, the sun's visibility from the point, shows black against lit. V
  depends only on the true point and the sun's direction.
- **The suns** (`look.rs`, `SUNS`) **[code]**:

  | | peaks at | disc | orbit | speed |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | main sun | 27° | 0.7° | 900 s | 0.4°/s |
  | dim sun | 16° | 4.5° | 347 s | 1.04°/s |

  Each orbit is a great circle, so a sun's direction is a function of one
  angle.
- **How low they are** **[computed]**:
  - the main sun is under 5° for 12% of its day and under 10° for 25%,
    median 18.7°;
  - the dim sun is under 5° for 20% and under 10° for 43%, median 11.2°.
- **The world is static and deterministic so far** **[code]**: generated
  from the seed, the same every time.
- **The ground's tops are a height field** (one top per x, z), with
  vertical walls, except where something overhangs: vault undersides,
  structures, the hanging cities.
- **Today's shadow systems** **[code]**:
  - **Bevy's cascaded maps** at 4096 (`look.rs`): the main sun 4 cascades,
    the first to 40 m, the last to 3 km; the dim sun 2 cascades, to 120 m
    and 1.2 km.
  - **The game's own soft shadows** (`sun_shadows.wgsl`).
  - **Cached far maps** (`far_shadow.rs`), drawn only by the coarse
    terrain levels: main sun to 1.8 and 5 km, dim sun to 2.2 km. Combined
    with the live ones by taking the darker.
  - **A shell mask** under the world (`shell.rs`).
  - **Rules for which level casts while two levels fade** (`terrain.rs`).
  - **Lamp shadows** limited to 8 stand-ins.
- **Levels of detail** (`terrain.rs`, `plates.rs` `mesh_column` and
  `visit_fitted`) **[code]**:
  - columns of 64, 256 and 1,024 m, drawn to 450 m, 1.7 km and 4.8 km;
  - the coarse levels are generated by their own rules: planes fitted
    within 3 px (1.70 m at level 1, 6.43 m at level 2), plain pillars,
    canal detail scaled by level;
  - they're drawn lowered (the sink: 1.7 and 8.1 m) with a depth bias, and
    dissolve into each other over 0.4 s, so two levels overlap during a
    switch;
  - colour and sky visibility are computed per level, so they can change
    when a level takes over.
- **Structures have no levels of detail** (`structures.rs`, `LAYERS`)
  **[code]**: sites drawn from 2.5 km and dropped at 2.9 km; colossi from
  4.5 km and dropped at 5 km. They cast only through the live cascades.
- **The planet bend** **[code]**: every terrain vertex is lowered by d²/2R
  round the camera, R = 40 km. This is done in the main pass **and in the
  shadow passes and far maps** (`terrain_prepass.wgsl`, `far_shadow.wgsl`),
  so shadows are computed in the world as bent round the current camera.
  Normals are not bent. A plate at distance d is tilted by d/R: 0.64° at
  450 m, 2.4° at 1.7 km, 6.9° at 4.8 km **[computed]**.
- **The user's decision on the bend:** display only. Lighting and shadows
  are to be computed on the flat world. This is the simplest choice, to be
  revisited if the look turns out wrong because of it.
- **Sizes** **[measured]**: triangles from `mesh_column`, seed 1, each
  district forced, over one 1,024 m square at each level (L0 / L1 / L2):

  | District | L0 | L1 | L2 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | floor | 68 k | 8 k | 2 k |
  | tiers | 215 k | 223 k | 12 k |
  | stacks | 4,670 k | 127 k | 69 k |
  | broken | 209 k | 94 k | 25 k |
  | uplands | 63 k | 9 k | 2 k |
  | highlands | 75 k | 11 k | 5 k |

  The grown pillar forms are about 95% of stacks at L0. In tiers, L1 is
  larger than L0.
- **Low sun and geometric error** **[computed]**:
  - **On the ground:** a caster's height error δ moves its shadow's edge
    by δ / tan(elevation): 5.7× at 10°, 11× at 5°, 29× at 2°.
  - **Relative to the penumbra:** the penumbra grows by the same factor,
    so the error matters as δ / (D · tan α), with D the distance from
    receiver to caster and α the sun's disc, whatever the elevation.
  - **So** a caster simplified by up to D · tan α (1.2 cm per metre for
    the main sun, 7.9 cm for the dim one) keeps its shadow within its own
    penumbra. A level of detail chosen by distance from the **camera**
    doesn't respect this; one chosen by distance from the **receiver**
    can.
- **Far pixels** **[computed]**: from a 2 m eye height at 1080p and a 90°
  view, one pixel covers about 0.37 × 83 m of flat ground at 450 m and
  0.8 × 409 m at 1 km. A shadow sampled at one point per pixel aliases
  there; far shadows need averaging over the pixel's footprint.

## 5. The user's other decisions

1. **A static world is not a commitment.** Changes in play (meteor
   craters) may come.
2. **The suns' orbits are not fixed by design.** They were chosen early on
   artistic merit, probably part of the look but untested, and change if
   something better is found.
3. **A budget for moving shadowed lights** (muzzle flash, tether,
   creatures' glow) **is acceptable.**

## 7. Practical notes

- **Places:** the default start is (1200, 900) (`look.rs`). A stacks view
  is at `--tp stacks_view`.
- **Captures:** `--shot PATH.png` renders once loaded and exits.
  `--opt bench` gives frame times, with `--set width=1920 --set
  height=1080`.
- **Time:** the HUD shows the two suns' elevations, from which the time
  can be worked out.
- **The image is graded to greyscale,** so colour-tinted debug views show
  only as darkening.
- **When a build fails to remove `target\fast\game.exe`,** the game is
  running. Ask the user, don't kill it.
